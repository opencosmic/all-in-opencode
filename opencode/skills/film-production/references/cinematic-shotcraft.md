# Cinematic shotcraft

Keep shotcraft renderer-neutral. A shot should have a clear purpose before it
has a look: establish space, introduce a subject, reveal information, create
pressure, follow an action, or land an emotional beat.

Describe the camera in terms that survive a renderer change:

- framing and subject scale at the start and end;
- lens intent and its effect on perspective, compression, and depth;
- camera movement, arc, speed, and whether the move leads or follows action;
- blocking, eyelines, screen direction, foreground/background relationships,
  and the staging that makes the action legible;
- duration, pacing, motion rhythm, and the intended transition into the next
  shot.

Prefer one readable camera idea per shot. Use a hold, acceleration, deceleration,
or motivated reveal deliberately; do not add movement merely to make a shot
feel cinematic. Record assumptions when exact staging, lens, or timing is still
uncertain so previs can resolve them.
